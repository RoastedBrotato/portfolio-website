# Voice sheet

The thesis, the enemy, and the words. Every surface on the site is written
against this page. `docs/design-audit.md` covers the visual system; this
covers what the site says.

## The thesis

> **Make it interesting. Make it work.**

Two beats. The first is the creative work: motion, 3D, the things people
remember. The second is the engineering under it: the site that is still up,
fast and maintainable a year later. The site is built so both beats are
visible, in that order, on every page.

The line generates the rest. When a surface needs a name, derive it:

| Surface | Derived from the thesis |
|---|---|
| Hero headline | Make it interesting. Make it *work.* |
| Selected work label | Make it interesting |
| The problems section label | Make it work |
| Closing CTA headline | Let's make it *work.* |
| Quote button | Get a quote (unchanged: it's the ad's call to action) |
| Engineering strip | The part that has to work |

The one headline gesture is the italic red word, and it is always the word
that carries the second beat ("work").

## The enemy

The site that looked great in the mockup and fell over after launch.

Named on the homepage as four symptoms, each a headline and one line:

1. Looks great. Runs terribly.
2. The feature works. Until you need to change it.
3. Everything depends on one fragile setup.
4. Paid once. Paid again to fix it.

The answer to the enemy is one person across the interface and the systems
behind it, so nothing is lost in a handoff. That is the only "why me"
argument the site makes. It does not list adjectives.

## Who is talking

First person, dry, specific. The blog is the reference voice, not the old
bio: "I've already done the bad-management thing" is the register. Say
"you" before "I". Name the problem the reader has before saying what I do
about it. A joke is fine where the reader is relaxed (404, form success,
the review form) and not where they are deciding (packages, quote form,
process).

The person is on the page: "I'm Waleed" appears once on the homepage, with
one line of texture, and the About page carries the rest.

## What the site sells

Websites. Landing pages, brand sites, and the motion and 3D on top. Anything
that isn't a website (software, automation, AI features, brand work) goes
through Sufun, my studio, and the site says so instead of offering a
"custom" option.

## Avoid / prefer

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Seamless | Explain what connects or works together |
| Streamlined process | Describe the actual steps |
| Without the headache | Name the problem you remove |
| Cutting-edge | Name the technology or capability |
| Digital experiences that convert | Explain the outcome and how you achieve it |
| Innovative solutions | Describe what you built |
| Passionate about technology | Show what you experiment with |
| Tailored solutions | Explain what changes for each client |
| End-to-end solutions | Specify which parts you handle |
| Results-driven | Show measurable results where available |
| Immersive (as a noun or a brand word) | Say what moves: motion, 3D, scroll, a scene |
| Distinctive, unique, bespoke | Describe the thing |

## Microcopy register

- 404: "Looks like we wandered off-trail. Even the best routes have detours. Let's get you back to the work."
- Quote sent: "Naisu. I'll read it tonight and reply tomorrow."
- Review form: "Drop your thoughts about me (or my work, haha)."
- Hero rail, desktop only: "The light follows your pointer. The frame rate holds."
